An Atlas of Discourse

Topic-Keyword Visualization

This visualization presents the archive as a structured, temporal grid where each article appears as a discrete unit—organized chronologically and annotated by dominant topic and word count. The design allows archive explorers to detect editorial rhythms, bursts of coverage, and gaps in discourse.

Features:

  • Organizational Logic: Chronological display of articles as units, mapped to a dominant topic cluster
  • Visual Metaphor: Timeline grid with topics and word count encodings
  • Navigation Method: Vertical scanning by time and topic
  • Analytical Perspective: Editorial rhythm and content evolution
  • Temporal Framing: Sequential and linear
  • Experience: Structured, pattern-based analysis
  • Strengths of Epistemic Invitation: Uncover unexpected connections across actors

Actor-Network Visualization

This visualization adapts Rodighiero and Daniélou's Weather Map (2023) , and re-conceptualizes the archive as a spatial, networked landscape. Articles are organized by the co-occurrence of named actors, resulting in a dynamic map of thematic clusters. Archive explorers can discover unexpected connections across interconnected narratives.

Features:

  • Organizational Logic: Spatial clustering of articles based on actor co-occurrence
  • Visual Metaphor: Topographic map with color coded clusters
  • Navigation Method: Lateral exploration via proximity of thematic clusters
  • Analytical Perspective: Networked discourse dynamics
  • Temporal Framing: Collapsed and simultaneous
  • Experience: Ambient, open-ended discovery
  • Strengths of Epistemic Invitation: Wander through resonant meaning, uncover unexpected connections across actors
